What the patient or caregiver sees

Every patient gets a text with a secure link at registration: on their own phone, or to family or their facility when they can't answer. Whoever answers enters the code, sees the list the hospital already has, and marks each medicine: still taking, stopped, or a different dose. They can add anything missing and photograph a bottle. Right after the code, they are asked the patient's weight, so the team has a stated weight to plan from while the patient waits for a scale.

No app and no account. The same link can go to a nursing facility. If nobody opens it, a reminder goes out and the technician's follow-up call is logged on the patient's line in the queue.

℞Med history queue
Ranked by risk, so the next patient is not picked by hand. Every patient gets a link at registration, to their own phone or to family or a facility. The technician works from the top: a confirmed list that matches the records goes straight to verification, and the bedside interview is saved for discrepancies, no response, or patients who can't answer.

Which alerts Ottley shows, and which it leaves out
Pediatric

Weight in kg, and whether it was measured or stated, by whom and when. A stated weight turns the banner amber and blocks finalizing until a measured weight is in. Every liquid shows mL, mg and mg/kg.

Weight · every patient

Asked on the link right after the code, and entered at the bedside when someone can weigh. Stated and measured are always labeled apart, with who and when. No weight at all shows red on the queue, because weight-based doses wait on it.

Height · every patient

When records are pulled, Ottley also looks for a height: a measured height from an earlier visit here, Care Everywhere, or a scanned ID in the chart. Each one says where it came from and when. If nothing turns up, it says so and asks for a stated or bedside height. IBW and BMI show only when both height and weight are in, labeled with what they were calculated from.

Pregnant · postpartum

Weeks, or postpartum day and breastfeeding. Prenatal vitamins, low-dose aspirin and meds started at delivery discharge are the usual misses.

Direct admit from surgery

Says plainly that no ED history was done. Each held home med shows when it was held and the last dose.

Veteran · Kaiser · facility

Says which records are out of reach and what to do instead: label photos, the Kaiser list date, the facility MAR time.

Teen

The link goes to the patient, not the parent, so confidential meds are not exposed.

Left out on purpose

No banner for age over 65 or for many meds (the risk score covers both), no repeat of a warning that is already red on the line, and no 3 a.m. page for anything that can wait for the day team.
